Hotels in Addis Ababa: Bole Stays, Guest Houses and Prices

Addis is one of the cheapest capital cities in Africa to sleep in, and Bole solves the two things most visitors care about: the airport and dinner.

Short answer

Stay in Bole. It holds the airport, most of the good restaurants and the widest range of hotels, and it is where almost every visitor ends up for good reason. Addis Ababa has around 180 bookable properties on EA Booking, from about $16 a night, with the median near $54.

Because Bole International is the hub of Ethiopian Airlines, a very large share of guests here are on a layover. If that is you, staying in Bole is not a compromise, it is the correct answer.

Bole and the airport

Bole runs from the airport north west towards the centre and contains most of what a short-stay visitor wants. Addis Ababa Bole International is the largest hub in Africa by passenger numbers, so the airport hotel market here is deeper and better than in most capitals, and prices stay reasonable because of the competition.

Best hotels in Addis Ababa

The top of the market here is good value by international standards. A well-run four or five star property runs $110 to $195, which in most capitals buys a mid-range room. At that level expect reliable hot water and power, a restaurant worth eating in, and airport transfers included or cheap.

Guest houses in Addis Ababa

The guest house market here is unusually strong and is where the value sits. These are small, often family-run properties with a handful of rooms, breakfast included and a level of personal attention that hotels at three times the price do not manage. Many are in quiet residential streets in Bole and Kazanchis, a few minutes from the main roads.

Cheap and budget hotels

Addis has the lowest entry price of any capital in this region. Rooms start at about $16 and there is a deep band between $19 and $50 that is perfectly comfortable. Getting around the city

Addis is large and spread out, and the light rail, while cheap, does not reach most places a visitor wants. Ride hailing works and is inexpensive by international standards, which makes staying slightly outside Bole more viable than it first appears.

Traffic is heaviest in the mornings and again from late afternoon, and the drive between Bole and the historic centre can double at those times. If your day is built around meetings in one district, book in that district rather than optimising on room price alone.

For a layover of under twelve hours, staying at or beside the airport is almost always the right call. For anything longer it is worth coming into the city, because Bole's hotel strip is convenient rather than interesting.

  • Altitude is real. Addis sits at roughly 2,300 metres. Take the first day gently, drink more water than feels necessary, and go easy on alcohol the first night.
  • Layovers are well catered for. If you have a long connection, the Bole hotels are minutes from the terminal and many offer free shuttles. Check whether your ticket includes a hotel, as Ethiopian Airlines provides one on some long connections.
  • Evenings get cold. The altitude means nights are chilly year round. Confirm heating or extra blankets at the budget end.

Paying for things

Card acceptance is improving but still patchy outside the larger hotels, so carry cash for taxis, smaller restaurants and anything outside Bole. What the altitude means in practice

At roughly 2,300 metres most visitors notice the first day: stairs are harder work and alcohol hits sooner. Take the first afternoon slowly and drink more water than feels necessary. Evenings are cold year round, which is worth confirming at the budget end where heating is not a given.

Where to go next

Addis is the connecting point for the whole northern historic route. From here you fly to Lalibela, Gondar and Bahir Dar, where inventory is much thinner and needs booking earlier. Entry is by e-visa, covered in our East Africa tourist visa guide.

Common questions

How much is a hotel in Addis Ababa?

From about $16 a night, with a median near $54. Comfortable mid-range hotels run $45 to $80 and the best four and five star properties are $110 to $195, which is good value by international standards.

Where should I stay in Addis Ababa?

Bole for almost everyone. It has the airport, the widest choice of hotels and most of the good restaurants. Kazanchis suits conference visitors and Piazza has the cheapest rooms in the old city.

Are there hotels near Bole airport?

Yes, a deep selection, from guest houses around $45 to the five star Ethiopian Skylight Hotel at the airport itself for about $193. Because Bole is Africa's busiest hub, the layover hotel market here is better than in most capitals.

What is a guest house in Addis Ababa like?

Small, often family-run, usually a handful of rooms with breakfast included, in quiet residential streets. They start around $16 and are the best value in the city, typically offering more personal service than hotels at several times the price.

Is Addis Ababa affected by altitude?

Yes. The city sits at roughly 2,300 metres, so most visitors feel it on the first day. Take it slowly, drink plenty of water and be cautious with alcohol on the first night. Evenings are cold year round.
