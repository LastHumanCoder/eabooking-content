Hotels in Kampala: Best Areas, Pool Stays and Cheap Options

Kampala is built across a set of hills, and which hill you stay on decides your evening. Here is what each area costs and who it suits.

Short answer

Stay in Kololo or Nakasero for the best hotels and the safest walking, Bugolobi or Ntinda for apartments and value, and the city centre only if you want to be close to everything at the cheapest price. Kampala has around 280 bookable properties on EA Booking, from about $31 a night, with the median near $109.

Traffic is the real constraint. A four kilometre journey can take an hour at the wrong time of day, so pick your area around where you will actually be.

Best hotels in Kampala

The top of the Kampala market is concentrated in Kololo and Nakasero, where the international brands and the better independents sit within a few minutes of each other. At this level you are paying for a pool, reliable power and water, secure parking and a restaurant you can eat in every night.

Hotels with a swimming pool

A pool is more useful in Kampala than the latitude suggests, because the city sits at around 1,200 metres and the afternoons are warm rather than brutal. Most four star properties in Kololo, Nakasero and Naguru have one, and it is generally the dividing line between the $60 and the $100 tiers. Apartments and longer stays

Kampala has an unusually deep serviced apartment market, concentrated in Bugolobi, Naguru and Kitante. For anything over three or four nights they typically beat a hotel room of the same standard, and they come with a kitchen, which matters when the traffic makes going out for dinner a project.

Cheap hotels and guest houses in Kampala

The budget tier is real and reasonably well distributed. Rooms start around $31 and there is a solid band of guest houses and small hotels between $31 and $60, mostly in Ntinda, Kisaasi and around the city centre. The trade-off is distance from the restaurant districts rather than any drop in safety.

What a night in Kampala actually buys

At the bottom of the range, around $31 to $46, you get a clean room, a fan or a basic air conditioner, and usually breakfast. Hot water can be a boiler that needs switching on half an hour ahead, and brief power interruptions are normal rather than remarkable.

Between about $59 and $85 the picture changes: reliable hot water, backup power, secure parking and a restaurant worth eating in more than once. This is the band most business visitors should book, and Urban Oasis at $59 shows how much further the money goes here than in Nairobi.

Above roughly $95 you are paying for a pool, a gym, a generator you never notice, and a location in Kololo or Nakasero where you can walk in the evening. Whether that is worth close to double depends on how many nights you are staying and whether you plan to leave the hotel after dark.

Entebbe, for your first and last night

The international airport is at Entebbe, about 40 kilometres away, and the road in can take well over an hour. Most travellers stay in Entebbe on arrival and again before departure, and put the Kampala nights in the middle. Practical notes

  • Plan around the traffic. Mornings until about ten and evenings from five are slow. Boda boda motorcycle taxis are the fast option and carry their own risks.
  • Prices include taxes. The figure shown on EA Booking is inclusive of taxes and fees.
  • Book ahead in December and during conferences. The good Kololo and Nakasero properties fill quickly.
  • Check for a generator. Power is generally reliable but interruptions happen, and the better hotels list backup power in their amenities.

When to book

Kampala fills around conferences and in December, and the good Kololo and Nakasero rooms go first. Outside those windows a week or two of notice is usually enough.

Where to go next

Kampala is a staging point rather than a destination for most visitors. From here the western circuit runs to Kibale, Queen Elizabeth and Bwindi, and the overnight stops are Fort Portal and Mbarara. Entry rules including the East Africa joint visa are in our visa guide.

Common questions

What is the best area to stay in Kampala?

Kololo and Nakasero have the best hotels, the quietest streets and the easiest walking. Bugolobi and Naguru suit longer stays and serviced apartments. Ntinda and the city centre are the cheapest, at the cost of distance from the restaurant districts.

How much is a hotel in Kampala?

From about $31 a night, with a median near $109. Good mid-range hotels run $75 to $130 and the top four star properties are around $125 to $195.

Are there cheap hotels in Kampala?

Yes. Rooms start around $31 and there is a wide band of guest houses and small hotels between $31 and $60, mostly in Ntinda, Kisaasi and the city centre.

Should I stay in Kampala or Entebbe?

Entebbe for the nights either side of your flights, because the airport is there and the road into Kampala is slow. Kampala for the days in between if your business or sightseeing is in the city.
