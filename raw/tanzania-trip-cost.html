Tanzania Trip Cost: Daily Budget for Safari, Beach and Kili

Tanzania is expensive in exactly one way and cheap in most others. Splitting the trip into its three parts makes the budget obvious.

Budget roughly $150 to $450 per person per day on safari, $50 to $150 a day on Zanzibar, and $2,000 to $4,000 in total for a Kilimanjaro climb. Tanzania is not expensive to eat, sleep or travel in. It is expensive to enter a national park, and that single line is what makes the country's reputation.

Hotels in Arusha start around $18 a night and on Zanzibar around $25. The park fees do not move regardless of which of those you pick.

What a safari day is made of

A safari day is expensive because four costs stack, and only one of them is under your control.

  • Accommodation, anywhere from $53 for a gateway hotel near the Serengeti to over $4,000 at the top camps. This is the line you choose.
  • Park fees, charged per person per 24 hours by TANAPA, and separately by the NCAA for the Ngorongoro Conservation Area. Descending into the crater adds a vehicle fee. Confirm current tariffs on their own sites.
  • Vehicle, fuel and guide, priced per vehicle per day, so four people pay half what two people pay each.
  • Food and water, usually included at camps and lodges on full board.

Zanzibar, where the budget recovers

A Zanzibar day costs a fraction of a safari day. Rooms in Stone Town start around $25, Paje around $40, and a good mid-range beach hotel sits near $100. Local food is a few dollars a plate, dala dala minibuses cost almost nothing, and the main expenses are activities: a spice tour, a dhow trip, diving at Mnemba.

A realistic mid-range Zanzibar day is $90 to $150 per person all in. On a tight budget, $40 to $60 is genuinely achievable. Detail on is Zanzibar expensive and Zanzibar hotels.

Kilimanjaro climb cost

A Kilimanjaro climb is priced as a package because it has to be. Park fees, rescue fees and hut or camping fees are charged for every day on the mountain, and you are legally required to climb with a licensed operator, guides and porters.

LevelTotal costWhat it means
Budget operator$1,800 to $2,200Larger groups, basic gear, thinner margins on staff pay
Mid-range$2,200 to $3,200Good equipment, better food, reasonable guide ratios
Premium$3,500 to $5,000+Private groups, extra acclimatisation days, top guides

Two things matter more than price. First, route length: a seven or eight day route has a far higher summit success rate than a five day one, because acclimatisation is the whole game. Second, porter treatment, which is the clearest signal of a decent operator. A quote far below the range above is usually funded by paying porters badly.

Budget separately for tips, which are substantial and expected, and for gear hire if you are not bringing your own. Base yourself in Moshi, where rooms start around $17 a night, for the nights either side.

Is Tanzania expensive to visit?

Not really, with one exception. Food, transport, city hotels and beach hotels are all cheaper than most mainstream destinations. Park fees are among the highest in Africa, and they are unavoidable if you want to see the Serengeti or the Ngorongoro crater.

The practical consequence: shorten the safari and lengthen the beach. Four safari days and seven beach days costs far less than seven and four, and most people enjoy it more.

How to bring the cost down

  • Travel in April, May or November. Rates fall sharply. See best time to visit Tanzania.
  • Sleep outside the parks and drive in each morning.
  • Join a group safari to split the vehicle four ways instead of two.
  • Do a camping safari. The park fees are identical, the beds cost a fraction.
  • Use Tarangire and Lake Manyara instead of a second Serengeti day.
  • Eat local. A plate of food is a few dollars away from hotel restaurants.

Common questions

How much does a Tanzania trip cost per day?

Around $120 to $200 per person per day on a budget safari, $250 to $450 mid-range, and $600 or more at the comfortable end. Zanzibar runs $40 to $150 a day depending on level, and cities $35 to $110.

Is Tanzania expensive to visit?

Food, transport and hotels are cheap by international standards. National park fees are among the highest in Africa and are unavoidable, which is what gives the country its expensive reputation. Spending fewer days in the parks and more on Zanzibar changes the total dramatically.

How much does it cost to climb Kilimanjaro?

Between about $1,800 and $2,200 with a budget operator, $2,200 to $3,200 mid-range, and $3,500 or more for premium. Park, rescue and camping fees are charged per day on the mountain and a licensed operator is a legal requirement. Tips are extra and expected.

Can you do a budget safari in Tanzania?

Yes. A camping safari with a shared vehicle, sleeping outside the parks where possible, in the April to May or November low season, brings a safari day down to roughly $120 to $200 per person. The park fees stay the same whichever way you travel.

What is the cheapest way to see Tanzania?

Fewer safari days and more Zanzibar days. Travel in the low season, join a group to share the vehicle cost, use Tarangire and Lake Manyara alongside one Serengeti visit, and stay in guest houses in Arusha and Moshi, where rooms start around $17 to $18.
