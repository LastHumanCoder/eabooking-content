Uganda National Parks Guide: Costs, Hotels and Budget Tips

Uganda is the cheapest serious safari country in East Africa, and the only one where you can track gorillas and chimpanzees in the same week.

Short answer

Uganda's headline parks are Bwindi for mountain gorillas, Queen Elizabeth for savannah game, Murchison Falls for the Nile, and Kibale for chimpanzees. Everything except the gorilla permits costs noticeably less than the equivalent in Kenya or Tanzania. Hotels in Kampala start around $31 a night, Mbarara from $20 and Fort Portal from $24.

The gorilla permit is the exception and the reason most people come. It is expensive, limited in number and sells out months ahead.

What it costs to enter

Park entry, activity permits and vehicle fees are all published by the Uganda Wildlife Authority, which is the only source worth trusting because the tariffs are revised periodically.

  • General park entry is charged per person per 24 hours and is substantially cheaper than the headline Kenyan and Tanzanian parks.
  • Gorilla permits are a separate, per-person, per-trek permit for a single hour with a habituated family. Numbers are strictly capped, so they sell out months in advance, particularly for the June to August and December to February dry seasons. Book the permit before you book anything else.
  • Chimpanzee tracking in Kibale is also permitted separately, at a fraction of the gorilla price, and is the best value primate experience in the region.
  • Vehicle and guide fees are charged on top, as everywhere in East Africa.

Plan the whole trip around gorilla permit availability. Everything else in Uganda can be arranged much closer to the date.

Where to stay

Kampala

The capital, and where most trips start. Around 280 bookable properties, from about $31 a night with a median near $109. Full breakdown on our Kampala hotels page.

Entebbe

Entebbe holds the international airport and sits on Lake Victoria about 40 kilometres from Kampala. Because the road in can be slow, most travellers spend their first and last night here rather than fighting traffic twice. Around 98 properties, from about $29.

Uganda on a budget

Outside the gorilla permit, Uganda is the cheapest country in this region to travel well.

  • Rooms are cheap everywhere. Mbarara from $20, Fort Portal from $24, Kampala from $31.
  • Park fees are lower than Kenya's or Tanzania's headline reserves.
  • Skip the gorillas and the trip halves in price. Chimpanzee tracking at Kibale costs far less and is superb. Queen Elizabeth and Murchison Falls give you a full savannah safari without a premium permit.
  • Travel by road. Distances are moderate and buses and shared transport are inexpensive.
  • Go in the rains. March to May and October to November are wetter, cheaper and much quieter. Gorilla trekking still runs, on muddier trails.

Backpacking Uganda

Uganda is one of the easier countries in the region to travel independently. Towns along the western circuit all have inventory under $50, the bus network is extensive, and the distances between parks are manageable in a day. Budget the gorilla permit as a separate, fixed expense and plan the rest around it.

What a gorilla trek is actually like

You walk, sometimes for twenty minutes and sometimes for four hours, through steep wet forest, and you get exactly one hour with the family once you find them. Reasonable fitness matters more than experience, porters are available and worth taking, and the trek goes ahead in rain, which is most days.

Planning your trip

A typical first trip is ten to fourteen days: Entebbe, Kampala, then a loop west through Kibale, Queen Elizabeth and Bwindi, returning via Lake Mburo. Common questions

What are the best national parks in Uganda?

Bwindi Impenetrable for mountain gorillas, Queen Elizabeth for savannah game and the Kazinga channel, Murchison Falls for the Nile and big game, and Kibale for chimpanzee tracking. Lake Mburo is the easiest to reach from Kampala.

How much does a Uganda trip cost?

Less than an equivalent trip in Kenya or Tanzania on every line except the gorilla permit. Hotels start around $20 to $31 a night depending on the town, park entry is cheaper, and road transport is inexpensive. The gorilla permit is a large, fixed, per-person cost on top.

When should I book a gorilla permit?

Months ahead. Permit numbers are strictly capped and the June to August and December to February dry seasons sell out first. Secure the permit before booking flights or hotels, because the rest of the trip has to fit around the trek date.

Where do I stay for Kibale chimpanzee tracking?

Fort Portal, which has around 38 properties from about $24 a night and is also the base for the crater lakes.

Is Uganda good for budget travel?

Yes, it is the best value country in East Africa outside the gorilla permits. Rooms under $50 are easy to find in every gateway town, park fees are lower than the Kenyan and Tanzanian headline reserves, and buses are cheap.
