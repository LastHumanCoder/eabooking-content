Zanzibar has a luxury reputation it only half deserves. The resorts are expensive. The island is not.

Short answer

No. Zanzibar is one of the cheaper beach destinations in the Indian Ocean, and considerably cheaper than the Maldives, Seychelles or Mauritius. Budget travellers manage on $40 to $60 a day, mid-range sits at $90 to $150, and only the resort tier passes $250. Rooms start around $25 a night in Stone Town and $40 at Paje.

The reputation comes from the handful of beachfront resorts charging $300 to $800. They are a small slice of the 400-plus properties on the island.

What things actually cost

  • A room in Stone Town: from $25 a night, with good boutique hotels at $85 to $125.
  • A room at Paje: from $40, with comfortable places around $63 to $123.
  • A beachfront room at Nungwi: $66 to $115 for well-rated mid-range, $175 to $350 for a four or five star all-inclusive.
  • Local food: a plate of pilau, biryani or grilled fish from a few dollars. The Forodhani night market in Stone Town is cheap and good.
  • Restaurant meal at a tourist place: $10 to $20 a head.
  • Dala dala minibus: under a dollar for most journeys.
  • Private taxi, airport to Nungwi: considerably more than the minibus and usually worth it with luggage.
  • Spice tour: a half day, modest cost, and one of the better value things to do.
  • Diving or snorkelling at Mnemba: the most expensive routine activity on the island.

What a week actually costs

Multiply the daily figures and a week resolves quickly. On a budget footing, two people sharing spend roughly $280 to $420 each across seven days, which covers a guest house a short walk from the sand, local food, dala dalas and a couple of activities.

Mid-range lands nearer $630 to $1,050 each: a beach hotel with a pool, restaurant meals most evenings, private transfers rather than minibuses, and a dhow trip or a dive.

The comfortable end passes $1,750 each, and almost all of that difference is the room. Food, transport and activities barely move between the three levels; what changes is whether you are sleeping on the beach or five minutes from it. That is the single most useful thing to know before booking Zanzibar, because it is the one line you control.

Three things drive a Zanzibar budget, and none of them is the island being pricey.

  1. Which beach you choose. Nungwi and Kendwa in the north cost more than Paje and Jambiani in the east, because the sea there is swimmable at any tide.
  2. When you come. December to February is peak. April and May, the long rains, can be half the price.
  3. Whether you book all-inclusive. Below about $150 a night, all-inclusive usually means a limited menu and local drinks. Eating out is often better and cheaper.

Zanzibar on a budget

  • Stay one street back from the beach. The beach is public. A room with a sea view costs double a room five minutes' walk away.
  • Base yourself at Paje or Jambiani rather than Nungwi. Cheaper rooms, cheaper food, same ocean.
  • Travel in April, May or November. Rates drop hard. Some small hotels close in April and May.
  • Eat where the staff eat. Local restaurants are a fraction of hotel restaurant prices.
  • Use dala dalas. Slow, crowded, and almost free.
  • Book activities locally rather than through the hotel desk, and agree the price before you start.
  • Skip all-inclusive at the low end and eat out instead.

Cheap all-inclusive and last minute deals

All-inclusive resorts on Zanzibar cluster in the north and north east around Nungwi, Kendwa and Matemwe. Genuinely good four star all-inclusive runs about $175 to $350 a night for two, and anything advertised well below that usually restricts the food and drink included, which is fine if you know it in advance.

Late availability does appear, particularly in the shoulder months, but Zanzibar is not a strong last-minute market: the best-value mid-range places fill early because there are not many of them. Searching a flexible date window beats waiting for a discount.

What pushes a Zanzibar budget up

Three things, in order. Booking December to February rather than the shoulder months, which can add half again to the same room. Choosing the north coast over the east, where the premium buys tide-independent swimming. And booking activities through the hotel desk rather than locally, which is convenient and consistently the most expensive way to do it.

How it compares

Against other Indian Ocean beach destinations, Zanzibar is the value option. It is far cheaper than the Maldives or the Seychelles at every level, and cheaper than Mauritius for mid-range travel. Within East Africa, a Zanzibar day costs a fraction of a safari day, which is exactly why the two are paired so often. Common questions

Is Zanzibar expensive?

No. Budget travellers manage on $40 to $60 per person per day, mid-range is $90 to $150, and only the resort tier passes $250. Rooms start around $25 a night in Stone Town, and it is considerably cheaper than the Maldives, Seychelles or Mauritius.

How much does a week in Zanzibar cost?

Roughly $280 to $420 per person on a budget footing, $630 to $1,050 mid-range, and $1,750 or more at the comfortable end, excluding international flights. Two people sharing a room is assumed.

What is the cheapest part of Zanzibar to stay in?

Stone Town, from about $25 a night, and Paje on the east coast from about $40. Nungwi and Kendwa in the north cost more because the sea stays swimmable at every tide.

Are all-inclusive resorts in Zanzibar good value?

At $175 to $350 a night for a good four or five star, yes. Below about $150 a night, all-inclusive usually means a limited menu and local drinks only, and eating out is often better and cheaper.

When is Zanzibar cheapest?

April and May, the long rains, when rates can fall by half. Some smaller hotels close during those months. November has short rains, low prices and mostly good weather, which makes it the best value month overall.
