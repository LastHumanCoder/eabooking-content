East Africa Tourist Visa: Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda in 2026

One permit covers three countries. Two of the region's biggest destinations are not in it. Here is how the pieces actually fit together.

Short answer

The East Africa Tourist Visa is a single multiple-entry permit covering Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda, valid for 90 days. You apply through the country you will enter first, and that country becomes your point of entry. Tanzania and Ethiopia are not part of it and need their own separate visas.

Kenya has also moved to an electronic travel authorisation system, which changes how entry works even for nationalities that were previously visa exempt.

How the joint visa works

  • It is multiple entry within the three countries, so you can move between them and return without reapplying.
  • It is valid for 90 days from entry.
  • You must enter through the country you applied to. If you apply through Kenya, you enter through Kenya first. Plan your first flight accordingly.
  • It is for tourism only. Business travel needs a different permit.
  • It does not cover work or long stays.

Apply through the official immigration portal of the country you will enter first. Fees and processing times are published there and change, so treat only those sites as authoritative.

Kenya

Kenya operates an electronic travel authorisation that applies to most visitors, including many nationalities that previously did not need a visa. It is applied for online before travel and is separate from the joint East Africa visa, so decide which route you are using before you book.

If your trip is Kenya only, the electronic authorisation is the simpler path. If you are combining Kenya with Uganda or Rwanda, the joint visa usually works out better. Apply through the official Kenyan travel authorisation portal.

Uganda

Uganda issues single-country tourist visas electronically and also participates in the joint East Africa visa. Apply through the Uganda immigration portal. If Uganda is your first country and you plan to add Kenya or Rwanda, apply for the joint visa here.

Rwanda

Rwanda is the most open of the three. Visitors of all nationalities can obtain a visa on arrival, and Rwanda also participates in the joint East Africa visa. Details are published by the Rwanda Directorate General of Immigration and Emigration.

Tanzania, and why it is separate

Tanzania is not part of the East Africa Tourist Visa and requires its own. That is a practical issue for anyone planning a Kenya and Tanzania safari, or a Serengeti and Masai Mara combination, because the joint visa does not help at that border.

Tanzanian visas are available electronically in advance and, for many nationalities, on arrival. Note that Zanzibar is part of Tanzania for visa purposes, so a Tanzanian visa covers it. Apply through the Tanzanian immigration e-visa portal.

If your trip is a safari and Zanzibar combination within Tanzania, you need one Tanzanian visa. If you pair Kenya's Masai Mara with Tanzania's Serengeti, you need two visas.

Ethiopia

Ethiopia runs its own electronic visa system and is not part of any regional arrangement. The e-visa is applied for online before travel through the official Ethiopian e-visa portal, and it is the only site you should use.

If you are transiting through Addis Ababa on Ethiopian Airlines and leaving the airport, check the current transit rules, which differ from a standard tourist entry. Our Ethiopia guide covers the rest of the trip.

Which permit suits which trip

If your whole trip is in one country, the single-country visa is simpler and cheaper, and the joint permit adds nothing. The East Africa Tourist Visa earns its cost the moment a second of its three countries is involved, because it is multiple entry and removes a border application mid-trip.

The most common mistake is buying the joint visa for a Kenya and Tanzania itinerary. Those are the two countries travellers most often combine, and Tanzania is precisely the one the joint visa excludes. That trip needs a Kenyan authorisation and a separate Tanzanian visa, and no regional permit covers both.

Apply through the country you land in first. The permit is tied to that point of entry, so a flight routing change after you have applied can invalidate it.

  • Passport validity. Six months beyond your date of entry is the usual requirement across the region.
  • Blank pages. Several countries require at least one or two blank pages.
  • Yellow fever certificates. Frequently required depending on your route and where you have travelled before arriving. Check the requirement for each country on your itinerary.
  • Onward travel. Proof of onward or return travel is sometimes requested at check-in or on arrival.
  • Only use official portals. There are many lookalike sites charging a premium for a form you can complete yourself. Every link on this page goes to a government site.
  • Apply early. Electronic systems are usually quick, but processing times vary and peak seasons are slower.

Visa rules, fees and eligibility change without much notice. Everything here is a starting point, not advice. Confirm the current position on the official government portal for every country on your route before you book anything.

Common questions

What is the East Africa Tourist Visa?

A single multiple-entry permit covering Kenya, Uganda and Rwanda, valid for 90 days and intended for tourism. You apply through the country you will enter first, and you must enter through that country.

Does the East Africa visa cover Tanzania?

No. Tanzania is not part of the joint visa and requires its own, available electronically in advance and on arrival for many nationalities. Zanzibar is part of Tanzania for visa purposes and is covered by a Tanzanian visa.

Do I need a visa for Kenya?

Most visitors need an electronic travel authorisation, applied for online before travel, including many nationalities that previously did not need a visa. If you are also visiting Uganda or Rwanda, the joint East Africa Tourist Visa may suit you better.

Can I get a Rwanda visa on arrival?

Yes. Rwanda offers visa on arrival to visitors of all nationalities, and it also participates in the joint East Africa Tourist Visa. Confirm the current position with Rwandan immigration before travelling.

How does the Ethiopia e-visa work?

It is applied for online before travel through Ethiopia's official e-visa portal. Ethiopia is not part of any regional visa arrangement. If you are transiting Addis Ababa and leaving the airport, check the separate transit rules.
