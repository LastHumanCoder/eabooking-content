Hotels in Kenya: Where to Stay, Park Fees and Budget Tips

A practical guide to stays across Kenya, from $17 rooms in Nairobi to beach hotels on the south coast and camps beside the Masai Mara, with the park fees that decide your real budget.

Short answer

Hotels in Kenya run from $17 rooms to $2,000 safari camps, and the price depends far more on where you are than on the standard of the room. Put another way, hotels in Kenya are cheap almost everywhere except beside the Masai Mara.

Kenya has hotels at every level, and the price depends far more on where you are than on the standard of the room. Nairobi rooms start around $17 a night, Mombasa around $26, Diani Beach around $22, and camps near the Masai Mara start around $74 and climb past $2,000.

Park entry fees are charged separately, per person per 24 hours, and are usually the second biggest cost after your stay.

Nairobi

Almost every Kenya trip starts and ends here. Nairobi has over 600 bookable properties and the widest price range in the country, with a median around $64 a night. Westlands suits most visitors, the CBD has the cheapest central rooms, and the airport hotels are worth it only for a pre-dawn flight. Full breakdown on our Nairobi hotels page.

The Masai Mara and the safari parks

The Mara is the reason most people come to Kenya, and it is the most expensive place to sleep in the country. Camps inside the reserve and in the bordering conservancies run from about $150 to well over $2,000 a night. Stay in a camp just outside the gate and the same standard of tent costs a third as much, at the price of a short drive each morning.

Kenya's other parks are much cheaper and far quieter. Amboseli has the Kilimanjaro views, Tsavo East and West are enormous and reachable from the coast, Lake Nakuru is an easy add-on from Nairobi, and Samburu in the north has species you will not see in the Mara. Kenya national park entry fees

Fees are charged per person for each 24 hours inside the park, on top of your accommodation and vehicle, and they differ for citizens, residents and non-residents. They are revised regularly, so the number to trust is the one on the authority's own site on the day you book.

  • National parks and reserves run by the state publish their rates through the Kenya Wildlife Service. This covers Amboseli, Tsavo, Lake Nakuru, Samburu and the rest.
  • The Masai Mara is different. It is a national reserve managed by Narok County, not by KWS, and it sets its own fee. The private conservancies along its northern edge charge an additional nightly conservancy fee per guest, which is often bundled into the camp rate.
  • Children and residents pay less. If anyone in your party holds Kenyan or East African residency, say so when booking. The difference is large.

Budget park fees as a separate line from day one. A family of four on a four-day Mara trip can easily pay more in entry fees than for two nights of accommodation.

Kenya on a budget

Kenya has a reputation as an expensive destination. That is true of the Mara in high season and not especially true of anything else.

  • Travel in April, May or November. These are the cheapest months and the parks are quiet. See best time to visit Kenya for what you trade.
  • Sleep outside the reserve. The single largest saving available on a Kenyan trip.
  • Swap one safari day for two coast days. Diani costs a fraction of a Mara camp per night.
  • Use the smaller parks. Tsavo and Amboseli deliver a full safari at a much lower fee and lodging cost than the Mara.
  • Share a vehicle. A private 4x4 is priced per car, not per person.

The coast

Kenya's coast is the country's best-value week. Diani Beach, south of Mombasa, is the headline: white sand, a reef, and a price range from $22 guest houses to $400 beach resorts. Mombasa itself is cheaper still, with a median around $51 a night, and works well as a base for Tsavo East. Watamu and Malindi further north are quieter.

Getting around

The Nairobi to Mombasa railway is comfortable, cheap and books out ahead of time. Road transfers between Nairobi and the Mara take five to six hours. Light aircraft transfers save most of a day and cost considerably more. Within Nairobi, ride hailing is the simplest option.

Planning your trip

A first Kenya trip usually runs nine to twelve days: two nights in Nairobi, three or four in the Mara or another reserve, and four or five on the coast. Common questions

How much is a hotel in Kenya?

Rooms start around $17 a night in Nairobi and $22 on the coast at Diani. A comfortable mid-range hotel is typically $50 to $90. Safari camps near the Masai Mara are a separate market, starting around $74 and running past $2,000 a night.

What are the best national parks in Kenya?

The Masai Mara for big cats and the migration, Amboseli for elephants and Kilimanjaro views, Tsavo East and West for scale and easy access from the coast, Lake Nakuru for flamingos and rhino, and Samburu in the north for species found nowhere else in the country.

How much are Kenya national park entry fees?

They are charged per person for each 24 hours in the park and vary by park, season and residency. The Kenya Wildlife Service publishes rates for the national parks, and the Masai Mara sets its own through Narok County. Always confirm the current figure on the official site before you budget.

Can you visit Kenya on a budget?

Yes. Travel in April, May or November, sleep just outside the reserves rather than inside them, use Tsavo or Amboseli instead of the Mara, and spend more of the trip on the coast. Done that way Kenya is no more expensive than many mainstream destinations.

How many days do you need in Kenya?

Nine to twelve days is the usual first trip: a night or two in Nairobi, three or four on safari and four or five at the coast. A safari-only trip works in five or six days.
